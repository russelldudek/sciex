First 120 days

Read the system. Prove the mechanism. Transfer ownership.

A discovery-led entry plan for global AI deployment and business process transformation.

Entry principles

Do not arrive with a platform answer. Start with business work, users, baselines and decision rights.

Use DBS as the deployment discipline. Make problems, countermeasures, measures and ownership visible.

Value and adoption are one system. A use case that is technically sound but behaviorally absent has not delivered.

Protect expert authority. Quality, regulatory, technical and commercial decision rights must be explicit.

Days 0-30

Read: establish one factual operating picture

Actions

  • Conduct Gemba-style listening across service, commercial, operations, quality, product management, IT, DBS and regional leaders.
  • Inventory active AI, analytics and automation initiatives, including Danaher platforms and vendor commitments.
  • Map intake routes, decision rights, dependencies, risk categories, current measures and sustainment ownership.
  • Review current steering committee purpose, agenda, thresholds and information burden.

Outputs

  • Enterprise AI portfolio map
  • Stakeholder and authority map
  • Use-case qualification rubric
  • Baseline/value evidence gaps
  • Governance charter hypothesis

Exit evidence

Leadership agrees on the current portfolio, decision boundaries and selection criteria. Unknowns are explicit rather than hidden inside status reporting.

Days 31-60

Resolve: select lighthouse workflows and define the method

Actions

  • Apply the AI Deployment Assay to the highest-value candidate workflows.
  • Select two or three lighthouse initiatives with different functional contexts but reusable deployment learning.
  • Create charters with business owner, user, baseline, AI role, human authority, risk, adoption plan and value measure.
  • Align Danaher, SCIEX IT and vendor responsibilities; expose integration and sustainment dependencies.

Outputs

  • Prioritized 12-month transformation portfolio
  • Lighthouse charters and roadmaps
  • Steering decision calendar
  • Adoption and communication plan
  • Risk and dependency register

Exit evidence

Each lighthouse has a named outcome owner, defined decision boundary, realistic adoption mechanism and explicit scale/hold/stop evidence.

Days 61-90

Run: deploy with DBS cadence and user evidence

Actions

  • Launch bounded pilots in existing workflows with frontline participation and visible daily/weekly management.
  • Track technical performance, workflow outcome, retained usage, exceptions, quality/risk signals and support burden.
  • Use short feedback loops to revise standard work, training, prompts, data or integration.
  • Bring decisions - not slideware - to the steering committee: continue, correct, hold or stop.

Outputs

  • Pilot operating boards
  • User feedback and exception log
  • Value evidence packet
  • Change-readiness readout
  • Scale/hold/stop recommendations

Exit evidence

At least one lighthouse demonstrates a credible chain from workflow change to adoption and measurable business response; failures produce reusable learning.

Days 91-120

Transfer: standardize what works and build capability

Actions

  • Move successful deployments into standard work, support, ownership, training and quarterly value review.
  • Retire or redesign initiatives that do not meet evidence thresholds.
  • Establish a reusable deployment playbook, community of practice and post-deployment owner network.
  • Define the transformation team needed after proof: portfolio, deployment, change/adoption and value realization capabilities.

Outputs

  • AI deployment operating model
  • Standard work and sustainment contracts
  • Community of practice charter
  • Capability and hiring plan
  • Next-wave portfolio recommendations

Exit evidence

SCIEX has a functioning governance rhythm, a tested deployment method, named sustainment owners and a portfolio that can explain why each initiative should scale, change or stop.

120-day scorecard

DimensionQuestionIllustrative evidence
Portfolio clarityCan leaders see what is active, why and who owns it?Coverage, duplication, dependencies, decisions due.
FlowDoes qualified work move with fewer avoidable delays?Intake-to-decision time, blocked dependencies, handoff completeness.
AdoptionAre intended users incorporating the capability into real work?Retained use, workflow adherence, feedback, training completion.
ValueIs the business response visible against a trusted baseline?Cycle time, rework, downtime, completeness, productivity or customer outcome.
ControlAre authority, exceptions and risks explicit?Guardrail coverage, escalations, quality findings, unresolved risks.
SustainmentCan the business own the capability after the project team leaves?Named owner, standard work, support path, review cadence.

Measures are categories, not invented targets. SCIEX baselines and thresholds should be established during discovery.
